PS-S01 EchoAssist: Intelligent Acoustic Analysis and Clinical Decision Support System Problem Statement Auscultation remains fundamentally an exercise in individual auditory judgment, wherein two trained listeners presented with an identical cardiac or respiratory recording may reach divergent interpretations, and subtle pathological cues risk being absorbed into ambient clinical noise before they are ever consciously registered. Existing digital stethoscope hardware has addressed capture fidelity, but the interpretive layer — transforming a waveform into a defensible, comparable, and revisitable clinical signal — remains largely unaddressed. This problem statement concerns the design of a system that processes raw acoustic recordings of heart or lung sounds, extracts clinically meaningful features, and produces classifications accompanied by a quantified confidence score and a traceable justification for that classification, without extending into diagnostic assertion. Noisy, motion-affected, and speech-interfered recordings should degrade the system's output gracefully rather than cause failure, and the scope for the core build is limited to a single sound domain (heart or lung), with the other domain treated as an optional extension. Dependencies • Access to labeled, sufficiently balanced heart/lung sound datasets (PhysioNet 2016 Challenge, PASCAL Heart Sound Challenge) for training, validation, and testing. • An audio-processing stack (e.g., Python with librosa/scipy or equivalent) capable of noise reduction, segmentation, and spectral feature extraction on variable-quality, real-world recordings. • A machine learning/deep learning framework (PyTorch, TensorFlow, or scikit-learn) combined with explainability tooling (e.g., SHAP, Grad-CAM adapted for spectrograms) to generate confidence scores and signal-level justifications. • Adequate compute resources (GPU preferred) to support iterative model training and experimentation within the project timeline. • Reference clinical knowledge on acoustic markers (S1/S2 boundaries, murmurs, wheeze/crackle patterns) to validate feature extraction and labeling, along with a dashboard/visualization framework for the end-to-end demo.Minimum Requirements • Noise-reduction pipeline applied to raw recordings before any analysis. • Feature extraction capturing at least the core acoustic markers (e.g., S1/S2 boundaries or wheeze/crackle segmentation, spectral features). • A trained classifier distinguishing normal from at least 2–3 abnormal categories, with confidence scores. • An explainability view highlighting which part of the signal drove the classification. • A working dashboard demoing the pipeline end-to-end on at least one real recording. • Reported evaluation metrics (accuracy, precision/recall, F1-score) across all classes to substantiate classifier performance. • Graceful handling of edge cases (silence, corrupted, or unusable audio) without pipeline failure.
